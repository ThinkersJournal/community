## Ideas swept
1. **Count-based auto-hide.** A post or comment with at least 3 distinct reporters in 24 hours is hidden from everyone but its author, pending a human review. The hide never expires. Only reports newer than the latest human ruling count. The hide and its audit row commit together (`moderation/auto-hide.ts`).
2. **Decision-atomic notice outbox.** The due-process notice is rendered and queued in the same transaction as the moderation decision. A cron drain claims rows with a lease and a fenced owner token, retries, and marks a row undeliverable after a horizon. Rows are kept for a configurable retention (`moderation_notice_outbox`).
3. **Per-purpose single-use action tokens.** A notice carries an `appeal` or `delete_request` link. The token is 32 random bytes, stored only as a SHA-256 hash, and consumed by one compare-and-set UPDATE. A page load only peeks, so mail-scanner GETs do not burn it. Mint, peek and consume refuse an anonymised account in the same statement (`moderation/action-tokens.ts`).
4. **Appeal window that starts at delivery.** The window runs from the outbox row's `delivered_at`, not from the decision. The drain shortens the links to `delivered_at + window`.
5. **DSA notice intake for unauthenticated reporters.** The reporter's email is validated. A target-less notice takes a content URL, which is resolved to a post id; a hidden post answers like a missing one (`routes/dsa-notice.ts`, `moderation/content-url.ts`).
6. **Upload scan with case-scoped quarantine** (designed and held, not live). A perceptual hash (PDQ port) of each upload is matched through "hash-matching service A". Only the matched keys are quarantined, a case holds the evidence, a human may confirm or clear, a CLEAR restores only what the case itself hid, and preservation runs at least one year past a report.
7. **Per-object legal hold on content-addressed media.** The hold is keyed on the storage key, so identical bytes in another visible post stay held. A held key is pulled from the public bucket, and access needs a two-person grant (`media/legal-hold.ts`).
8. **Account legal hold gating anonymisation.** An active hold blocks the in-place scrub. A banned account's email is reserved by a keyed HMAC (`auth/anonymise-accounts.ts`).
9. **Enforcement ladder.** Warn, suspend and ban are separate from the content decision. A pure `suggestNextRung(history)` suggests the next rung from the account's history. Actions older than 12 months stop escalating. The human moderator applies the action (`moderation/account-actions.ts`).
10. **Idempotency key on post creation.** A per-author key plus a SHA-256 of the request returns the stored result on a retry (`routes/posts.ts`).
11. **Realtime notification bell and per-post live engagement** over Durable Objects and WebSockets.
12. **KV cache of a user's followee list** for feed assembly.
13. **Tag-based edge-cache purge** on write, with stale-while-revalidate.
14. **Editor prefill from a URL fragment** (`/new-post#cr=<base64url JSON>`), with an idempotency key carried in the payload.
15. **Login challenge** shown after failed attempts.
Not searched (commodity or standard-practice, see Limitations): search and discover feed, tags, markdown sanitising and syntax highlighting, email digests, media garbage collection, signup and password reset.

## Needs review (to the PM)
1. **Hit 9, US9967266B2 (Flipboard), idea 6, the upload scan.** Not live and held, so nothing infringes today. Before the scan is released, someone who can read claims should decide whether "analyse image signals, suspect, verify with a verification service, act" reads on "hash locally, match through service A, hold, human review". If it does, the design-around to cost out is to avoid a distinct suspicion step before the external match (the design already has no local pre-filter) and to say so in the design. Also open: whether the matching service's own terms indemnify us.
2. **Hit 10, US9614850B2 (Microsoft), idea 9, the enforcement ladder.** Live in the moderation queue code. The overlap is the incident history record, the rule applied to it, and the account privilege change. The option space for CireSnave: (a) accept the risk, (b) remove `suggestNextRung` so no rule is applied to the incident history by code, leaving the moderator to read the history themselves, (c) have counsel read claim 1. I recommend (a) or (c) over (b), because (b) removes a useful aid for one operator to avoid a possible overlap I cannot confirm.

## Limitations
- AI-assisted search and read, by one lane in one session. It is not legal advice and not a freedom-to-operate opinion; a lawyer's review is a separate, later purchase.
- Search coverage is **thin**: about one query per idea, on Google Patents through a general web-search tool, so ranking is the tool's, not a patent search engine's. No USPTO, Espacenet or WIPO queries were run. No classification-code (CPC) search, no assignee-driven search (Meta, Google, Microsoft, Cloudflare, Trust & Safety vendors), no citation chasing.
- Claims were summarised by a fetch tool from the Google Patents page. I read the independent claims only; dependent claims were not checked except for hit 9. Where I could not see claims 1-10 of a patent (hit 5) I said so. Legal status and expiry come from Google Patents and can be wrong; they ignore terminal disclaimers, term adjustments and maintenance-fee lapses I did not verify. Pending applications and applications published since the pages were indexed are not covered.
- Ideas 11 to 15 and the unsearched list were searched once or not at all; they are well-known engineering patterns, but "commodity" is my judgment, not a finding.
- Third-party components bring their own patents that this file does not cover: PDQ (the perceptual hash), PhotoDNA and hash-matching service A (licensed under that service's terms), Cloudflare Workers, Durable Objects, KV, Hyperdrive, R2 and Turnstile (licensed under Cloudflare's terms), Postmark, and the open-source libraries in `pnpm-lock.yaml`. CodeRipper's `patent-index-match` rule covers dependencies and was not run for this file.
- The DSA and NCMEC compliance mechanisms are mandated by law; a patent on a mandated process is a legal question for counsel, not for this sweep.
