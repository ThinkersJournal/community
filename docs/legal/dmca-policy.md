We respect intellectual property and expect our users to do the same. This policy
describes how to report claimed copyright infringement on Thinker's Journal and how
we respond, consistent with the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA),
17 U.S.C. §512.

## 1. Reporting infringement (takedown notice)

If you believe content on the Service infringes your copyright, send a written
notice to our Designated Agent that includes all of the following (§512(c)(3)):

1. Your physical or electronic **signature**.
2. **Identification of the copyrighted work** you claim was infringed.
3. **Identification of the infringing material** and enough information for us to
   locate it (e.g. the post URL).
4. Your **contact information** (address, phone, email).
5. A statement that you have a **good-faith belief** the use is not authorized by
   the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
6. A statement, **under penalty of perjury**, that the information is accurate and
   that you are the owner or authorized to act on the owner's behalf.

## 2. Our response

On receiving a valid notice, we will **remove or disable access** to the identified
material within a reasonable time, and make a good-faith effort to **notify the user**
who posted it, providing a copy of the notice.

## 3. Counter-notification

If your content was removed and you believe it was a mistake or misidentification,
you may send our Designated Agent a written counter-notice including (§512(g)(3)):

1. Your **signature**.
2. **Identification of the removed material** and the location it appeared.
3. A statement, **under penalty of perjury**, that you have a good-faith belief the
   material was removed by mistake or misidentification.
4. Your **name, address, and phone**, and a statement consenting to the jurisdiction
   of the federal court for your district (or, if outside the U.S., [[JURISDICTION]]),
   and to accept service from the complainant.

When we remove material we email its author a copy of the notice and a link for sending
a counter-notice. If we receive a valid counter-notice we send it to the original
complainant and **restore the material 10 business days later** unless the complainant
tells us before then that they have filed a court action seeking to restrain the
activity. We do not restore material if a moderator has since ruled on it, or while
the author's account is under a legal hold.

## 4. Repeat infringers

Consistent with §512(i), we maintain a policy of **terminating, in appropriate
circumstances, the accounts of users who are repeat infringers**. Every removal
made on a copyright notice is recorded against the author's account. A removal
counts while it stands: one that is restored (after a counter-notice, or because the
complainant withdrew) or that we rejected does not. When an account has **three or
more standing removals within twelve months**, it is flagged for a moderator to
review. The count and the flag are an aid to a person: **no account is terminated
automatically**. A moderator decides whether to terminate it, weighing the record
together with the rest of the account's history and the
[Community Guidelines](community-guidelines.md).

## 4a. Records

We keep a copyright case, including the details of the complainant and of the
author, for three years after it closes. After that we erase the names, addresses,
phone numbers, email addresses and signatures from it and keep only the case's
number, dates and outcome.

## 5. Trademark and other complaints

For non-copyright intellectual-property concerns (e.g. trademark, impersonation),
contact contact@thinkersjournal.com; these are handled under the
[Community Guidelines](community-guidelines.md).
