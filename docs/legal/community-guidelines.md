# Community Guidelines

**Version:** 2026-10-15 · **Effective:** 2026-10-15

Thinker's Journal is a community for people who build and think in public. These
Guidelines say what is not allowed and what happens when something breaks them.
They apply to everything you post: writing, comments, images and your profile.

## 1. What is not allowed

You may not post, upload or link to content that:

1. **Is illegal**, or helps someone do something illegal.
2. **Sexually exploits or endangers children** in any way. This is zero tolerance:
   such content is removed and the account is banned.
3. **Threatens, harasses, bullies or incites violence** against a person or group.
4. **Is hate speech**: attacks or dehumanizes people based on race, ethnicity,
   national origin, religion, disability, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation
   or serious disease.
5. **Is non-consensual intimate imagery**, or sexualizes a real, identifiable
   person without consent.
6. **Doxxes**: publishes private information (home address, phone, government ID,
   financial details) about someone without their consent.
7. **Is spam**: bulk, repetitive, deceptive or purely promotional posting; SEO
   manipulation; engagement farming.
8. **Distributes malware, phishing or scams**, or tries to compromise the service
   or other people's accounts.
9. **Impersonates** a person or organization, or misrepresents your affiliation.
10. **Infringes intellectual property**: posting someone else's work as your own,
    or without the right to do so. Rights holders can report it as described in
    section 5.

## 2. Reporting content

- A signed-in member with a verified email address can report a post or a comment.
  The report picks one reason: spam, harassment, hate, sexual content, violence,
  intellectual property or other.
- Each person can report a given post or comment once. Repeating the report changes
  nothing.
- One report does not hide or remove anything by itself. Reports are what moderators
  and the automatic hide below work from.

## 3. What happens next

**Automatic hide.** When one post or comment has been reported by **several different
members within a short time**, it is hidden from public view automatically, without a
moderator having looked at it yet. Its author is emailed that it is hidden pending
review. Hidden is not removed: a moderator then reviews it and either keeps it
hidden, removes it, or restores it. Reports made before a moderator's ruling on that
content do not count towards a new automatic hide.

**Moderator decisions on content.** A moderator can keep a post or comment hidden,
remove it, or restore it. When a moderator hides, removes or restores content, we
email the author the outcome and the moderator's written reason.

**Moderator decisions on accounts.** A moderator can:

- send a **warning**, which does not restrict the account;
- **suspend** the account for 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days: you cannot sign in until
  the suspension ends;
- **ban** the account: it is barred permanently and you cannot sign in.

Moderators choose according to how serious the violation is and what has happened on
the account before. They do not have to start with a warning. We email the account
holder the moderator's written reason.

**Record.** Moderator decisions are written to an internal log that cannot be edited
or deleted afterwards, so that a decision can be explained and appealed.

## 4. Appeals

- You can appeal a moderator's decision to keep your content hidden, to remove it, to
  warn you, to suspend you or to ban you, **within 30 days** of the decision.
- The email we send about the decision carries the appeal link. For a post a moderator
  has hidden, the post's editor page also shows an "Appeal this decision" link. A
  suspended or banned account can still use the link from the email.
- Each decision can be appealed once.
- A moderator reviews the appeal and records the outcome with a written reason, and we
  email it to you. If the appeal is granted, the content is restored or the
  suspension or ban is lifted.
- Content hidden by the automatic hide has no decision to appeal yet. If a moderator
  then keeps it hidden or removes it, you can appeal that decision.

## 5. Reporting illegal content without an account

You do not need an account to report illegal content. Use
[the report form](/dsa-notice):

- You give your name, your email address, the reason, a statement, and confirm the
  report is made in good faith.
- We email you a link to confirm your address. Until you follow it the report is not
  reviewed, and an unconfirmed report is discarded after 7 days.
- A confirmed report goes to a moderator's review list. **It never hides content
  automatically.**
- When a moderator decides, we email you the outcome and the moderator's reason.

## 6. Blocking another member

You can **block** another member. A block is about **interaction, not invisibility**.

- The person you block cannot follow you, comment on your posts or replies, or react
  to your content. Any existing follow between you is removed, in both directions.
- Their posts no longer appear in your feed, and notifications between you are
  suppressed.
- **A block does not make your public posts private.** Your posts stay public, so a
  blocked person may still read them, for example while signed out. Blocking is not
  a way to hide public writing from a specific person.

If someone's behaviour breaks these Guidelines, report it: blocking manages your own
experience, and reporting is how content is reviewed.

## 7. Your responsibilities

- Post your own work, or work you have the right to share, and credit others
  honestly.
- Report content you believe breaks these Guidelines, rather than escalating it
  yourself.
- Keep your account secure; you are responsible for activity under it.

## 8. Changes

We may update these Guidelines. The version and effective date at the top change
whenever the text does, and the emails we send about moderation decisions name the
version and link to this page.
